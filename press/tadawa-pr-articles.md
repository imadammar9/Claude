## 1. Tadawa relaunches tadawa.com as Jordan's digital front door to primary care

**AMMAN, Jordan — November 2026.** Tadawa Healthcare, the Jordanian telemedicine and primary-care company, today launched a fully rebuilt website that puts a family doctor one tap away for patients, and one page away for insurers, employers and healthcare providers.

The new bilingual site (Arabic and English) guides every visitor to the right level of care: an on-demand video consultation through *Treat Me Now*, a scheduled remote visit, a clinic visit in Amman, Tafileh or Irbid, or a home visit. A searchable help center, a doctor-reviewed health library and a symptom guide help families decide when a video call is enough and when the emergency room is the right choice.

"Most healthcare journeys in Jordan start at the wrong door," said Imad Ammar, Business Development Manager at Tadawa. "We built the site the way we built the service: a family doctor first, then everything else in its right place."

For organizations, the site publishes Tadawa's evidence openly, including a pilot with MedNet in which the average cost per medical form fell 46%, and a cost per visit of JD 18.59 against JD 31.80 at a public emergency department.

Tadawa was founded in Amman in 2022 and now employs more than 40 healthcare professionals, including more than 15 family medicine specialists who cover the app in shifts around the clock.

## 2. MedNet pilot: family-doctor gatekeeping cuts cost per medical form by 46%

**AMMAN, Jordan — November 2026.** An evaluation with third-party administrator MedNet shows that routing insured members to a Tadawa family doctor first reduced the average cost per medical form from JD 65 to JD 35, a 46% reduction across the pilot account.

The result comes as health claims paid in Jordan rose 16.9% year on year and the projected medical cost trend for the year reached 15%, up from 5% in 2025.

Tadawa's model replaces self-referral with a clinically owned routing decision. Members see a family doctor by video within minutes; specialists, labs and imaging are ordered only when clinically needed, and every prescription and referral is logged against a documented consultation that the insurer can verify.

Across Tadawa's own data, 80% of visits billed only the consultation, 77% of members needed a single visit, and one in five visits received emergency-type treatment such as IV fluids, nebulizer or ECG at clinic prices.

"Insurers do not need another app. They need a gatekeeper their members actually like using," said Imad Ammar. "The pilot shows both can be true at once."

Tadawa currently works with more than ten insurers and TPAs, including MedNet, GIG, Medexa, NatHealth, MedService, Omni Care, Solidarity, Euro Arab and Newton.

## 3. Tadawa Medical Center brings specialist care to Tafileh, open until midnight every day

**TAFILEH, Jordan — November 2026.** Families in Tafileh no longer need to travel to Amman or Karak for a specialist. Tadawa Medical Center, on Government Hospital Street in Al-Ais, is open from 8:00 in the morning until midnight seven days a week, including Fridays, with visiting specialists in ophthalmology, cardiology, chest medicine, neurology, surgery and rehabilitation.

The center combines family medicine with urgent care on site: ECG, IV fluids, nebulizer treatment, injections and wound care are available throughout opening hours, and after midnight a Tadawa doctor remains reachable through the app's *Treat Me Now* service.

Ten insurers and third-party administrators are accepted. Patients bring their insurance card and the reception handles eligibility and approvals.

"A family doctor who knows you and a specialist who comes to you: that is what Tafileh asked for, and that is what we opened," said Imad Ammar.

The center is Tadawa's second physical location after its family doctor clinic in Amman, alongside a partner clinic in Irbid run with Nabd Al-Amal Medical Clinics. Aqaba is next on the company's expansion plan, followed by Karak, Ma'an and Zarqa.

## 4. Tadawa launches employer plan: a family doctor for every employee and their family, 24/7

**AMMAN, Jordan — November 2026.** Tadawa today introduced a dedicated plan for employers that gives staff and their dependants a family doctor on call around the clock, with clinic and home visits when needed and sick leave issued only after a documented consultation.

A video consultation takes about 15 minutes instead of a day away from work. Employers receive monthly anonymised utilisation reports, a dedicated account manager, app training for teams and optional on-site medical days. In Tadawa's data, children account for 43% of visits and spouses for 15%, showing that families use the benefit, not only employees.

"Absenteeism, unverified sick leave and high-cost chronic claims are the three things HR teams raise with us," said Imad Ammar. "One family doctor who follows the whole household addresses all three."

The plan sits in front of an employer's existing insurance: where the insurer or TPA already works with Tadawa, the service becomes the first stop and claims fall; where it does not, Tadawa offers a direct corporate plan. Rollout takes days, with membership codes issued from the employer's staff list.

Employers can request a proposal at tadawa.com or by email to imad.ammar@tadawa.com.

## 5. Tadawa's free medical days reach universities and professional associations across Jordan

**AMMAN, Jordan — November 2026.** Tadawa's family doctors have taken part in a series of free medical days this year, offering on-site consultations, blood pressure and glucose checks and app onboarding to students, members and employees across the Kingdom.

Recent stops include the German Jordanian University and the Jordan Engineers Association in Amman, as well as community events in Tafileh, where the company operates its medical center. Visitors leave with a Tadawa account, a family profile for their household and a doctor they can reach by video the same evening.

"Awareness is part of primary care," said Imad Ammar. "If people know that fever, flu, a child's cough or a prescription renewal can be solved in fifteen minutes by video, they stop queuing in emergency rooms for things a family doctor should handle."

A 2025 study of 9.16 million visits to Jordan's public emergency departments found that 62% were low-acuity and 93% of patients were treated and sent home. Tadawa's model is designed to divert exactly that volume to primary care.

Universities, associations and employers interested in hosting a medical day can contact Tadawa through tadawa.com. Tadawa now serves more than 6,000 registered users and has delivered more than 3,500 consultations by video, in clinic and at home.
